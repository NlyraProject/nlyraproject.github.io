      Section 1

      1 · Getting started

      There is no sign-up, no account and no deposit. You connect a wallet and you trade. Everything below takes about three minutes.

      What The Desk is

      A trading terminal for Robinhood Chain, built around one uncomfortable fact: around 92 % of the tokens ever launched on this chain are dead. So every screen puts the deployer's record, the contract's powers and the holder concentration in front of you before the buy button, and the trading tools — limit orders, ladders, martingale, bots — exist so you do not have to sit and watch a chart to act on any of it.

      It is non-custodial in the literal sense. Market orders and limit orders never move your money anywhere except through the swap itself. The strategies that do hold funds hold them in a contract that only your wallet can empty — see §14.

      Step 1 · A wallet

      1. Use an injected wallet

        MetaMask, Rabby, or the built-in browser of any wallet app. There is no WalletConnect and no e-mail login. If none is found you get: No wallet detected. Open nlyra.xyz inside your wallet app, or install MetaMask / Rabby.

        On a phone, open nlyra.xyz inside your wallet app's own browser. Safari and Chrome on iOS cannot reach a wallet app's signing keys, so the connect button will find nothing there. Every wallet app has a browser tab — use that one.

      2. Press CONNECT, top right

        Approve the connection. Nothing is sent anywhere; the page just learns your address. Once connected the chip shows your ETH balance and your @name (or a shortened address).

      Step 2 · Robinhood Chain

      Everything happens on Robinhood Chain, chain id 4663. If your wallet is on another network, The Desk offers to switch — and if the network is not in your wallet at all, it offers to add it with these exact settings:

      Network nameRobinhood Chain
      Chain ID4663

      RPC URLhttps://rpc.mainnet.chain.robinhood.com

      If the switch does not take, you get Switch your wallet to Robinhood Chain and the wallet chip turns amber. Every transaction re-checks the network before it is built, and anything carrying the wrong chain id is refused before your wallet ever opens.

      Step 3 · ETH on Robinhood Chain

      ETH on Ethereum, Arbitrum or Base is not ETH on Robinhood Chain. It has to be on this chain before you can do anything. This is the single most common reason a new user gets stuck.

      Three ways in:

      Bridge

      From Arbitrum or Ethereum via portal.arbitrum.io/bridge or robinhood.com/chain.

      From the Robinhood app

      Buy ETH there and withdraw it to Robinhood Chain.

      Ask

      The group will walk you through it.

      While your balance is under 0.001 ETH with no USDG, a card appears on the board and in the trade panel telling you so and offering those three routes. It is a card, never a gate — every control stays live behind it — and you can dismiss it for 24 hours.

      How much you needGas on this chain is about $0.007 per transaction, so a very small amount goes a very long way. The minimum order for limit orders, ladder rungs and bot fills is 0.00085 ETH — roughly $2. Market swaps have no minimum beyond what the pool will accept.
      Keep a little backThe MAX button in the trade panel always leaves 0.0002 ETH behind for gas — about seventy transactions' worth — and tells you so.

      Step 4 · Your first trade

      1. Find a token

        Browse the MARKET board, watch new launches on FIREHOSE, or paste a contract address into the search box and press Enter.

      2. Read the verdict before the chart

        The chip in the header and the one line under it tell you what the contract can do to you. §3 explains every check.

      3. Type an amount and press BUY

        The first press connects your wallet; the second buys. Full walkthrough in §4.

      After your first fill you will see, once: You're on. Pick a token and tap BUY — nothing else to set up.

      Step 5 · Claim a @name

      Optional, and not required to trade — but it is your identity on the tape, the leaderboards and copy trading, and your referral link does not exist without one.

      1. Open the claim sheet

        From your account, or from the prompt that appears once after a fill.

      2. Type a name

        3 to 20 characters, letters, numbers and underscore only, and it may not start or end with an underscore. Availability is checked against the chain as you type: checking… → available or taken.

      3. Claim it

        One transaction. The name itself is free — you pay only chain gas, about a cent. It is registered on-chain, one per wallet, first come first served. You are @yourname on Robinhood Chain.

      Case does not create a second name. The registry stores your capitalisation for display but matches names lowercased, so Architect and architect are the same identity. There is a rename function on the contract with a 7-day cooldown, but The Desk does not expose it — treat the name you pick as permanent.

      Step 6 · Referrals

      Your link is nlyra.xyz/desk/?r=yourname, and any token page you share is automatically tagged with it once you have a name.

      What they getEveryone who arrives on your link pays 0.9 % instead of 1 % on every trade, permanently.
      What you get30 % of every fee they ever pay, credited to you on-chain by the fee router. Not a database entry of ours — a balance in a contract you can read yourself.
      How the link sticksFirst touch wins and it lasts 30 days in the visitor's browser. An existing referral is never overwritten by a newer link. Once they trade, the referrer is written into the router permanently and can never be changed by anyone, including us.
      ClaimingFrom the referral card in your account. It shows Referred, Earned and Claimable read straight off the chain, and CLAIM sends up to three transactions to sweep what is owed in each asset. Claimed 0.0214 — it is in your wallet.
      Self-referral does not workDropped at both ends. A referrer who has not claimed a @name is also refused — you get That referral link is not registered — trading at the standard fee and the trade proceeds at 1 %.
      One honest limitation. On this chain's V4 pools, fills executed by the keeper — limit orders, ladder rungs, martingale buys — pass no referrer, because the router would otherwise credit the contract instead of the person. The fee is charged as normal, but the 30 % share is not paid on those particular fills. Market swaps and fills on V2/V3 pools do credit the referrer normally.

      One signature, no gas

      Some things are private reads — your open orders, your ladders, your martingale cycles, your bot list, your alert feed. Anyone could otherwise enumerate a wallet's open orders just by knowing its address, so those reads are signed.

      Buy pressure, exactly

      Buy pressure is a whole number from 0 to 100: the percentage of recent flow in that token that was buying. It is drawn as a fill bar whose width is the number, with the figure next to it.

      72 % — greenat or above 55, buyers are in front
      50 % — grey46 to 54, no side is winning
      31 % — redat or below 45, sellers are in front
      — (dash)no flow data. It is never faked as 0.
      Buy pressure is a count of flow, not a prediction. A token with 100 % buy pressure and $600 of liquidity means a handful of small buys and nothing else. Read it next to LIQ and VOL or it will mislead you.

      The creator record

      Every row carries the launch history of the wallet that deployed the token. Three states:

      • — — no record on file.
      • first launch — this deployer has launched once. Not a good sign or a bad one; simply no history.
      • 9/24 with a bar (mobile prints it as 24×/38%) — 9 of this deployer's 24 launches are dead. The bar's width is the dead percentage.

      The number and bar turn hot (gold, with a skull on mobile) when the deployer meets the bad-record rule: at least 3 launches, at least 60 % of them dead, and not an official/verified deployer. That same rule is what the FLAGGED filter uses.

      ⚡ Quick buy

      The bolt at the right edge of a row buys immediately at market, without opening the token page. The chip labels itself with the size of the trade — BUY $25 — so you always know what you are about to spend before you press it.

      How much it spendsAlways 0.01 ETH (the first amount preset in the trade panel). On the WETH row it spends 10 USDG instead.
      The dollar label0.01 ETH converted at the live ETH price, then rounded to a figure a person would say out loud — nearest $5 below $100, nearest $10 above. If no ETH price is known the chip falls back to a bare bolt.
      What you seeA toast: Buying 0.01 ETH of PEPE…

      The ticker

      The scrolling strip is the chain's latest fills — not only Desk fills. Each chip reads who · side · size · token · age: @alice bought $420 PEPE 12s. Buys and sells are coloured differently, chips link to the token, and the newest three glow briefly.

      • Put a finger on it and it stops. Someone touching a chip is someone reading it.
      • It holds 40 chips and moves at a constant speed regardless of how many there are.
      • A chip is patched in place when the server later works out whose fill it was — it never jumps.
      • On a fresh visit the last snapshot is restored only if it is under 5 minutes old. A fill from an hour ago is not "just traded", so the strip stays empty instead.
      • It appears on MARKET, FIREHOSE and WATCH only.
      • With reduced motion switched on it does not scroll — it becomes an ordinary swipeable strip.

      How the board stays live

      PollingMarket every 15 s, firehose every 30 s, stats every 2 min, rap sheet every 5 min — and four times slower than that while the live socket is healthy, because the socket is already pushing. Everything pauses while the tab is hidden and refreshes the moment you come back.
      Price flashA cell whose price changed flashes green or red and fades back. Capped at 24 flashes per repaint: a whole board repricing at once would be a strobe.
      Just tradedOn mobile, a row whose volume moved gets a brief tint. Capped at 12 per repaint, and switched off entirely once you have scrolled.
      ↑ N new tokensOnce you have scrolled past the top, the board stops reordering under you. Prices keep updating in place, and a pill appears telling you how many new tokens are waiting (or list reordered). Tap it, or scroll back to the top, to let them in.
      Row capEvery board shows at most 150 rows after filtering. There is no pagination — narrow the filter or use search.

      The verdict — what it actually checks

      The verdict is the output of a contract scan. It is a risk score where higher is worse, banded into labels:

      DANGER45 and upOne or more findings that can cost you the position.
      BLUE CHIPno scoreA canonical asset — see below. No number is shown, deliberately.
      UNSCANNED—Never scanned. It predates the watch or has never been called.
      UNKNOWN—A stored verdict we could not interpret.

      A serial launcher also affects the verdict directly: a deployer with 5 or more launches and 80 % or more of them dead forces the verdict to at least CAUTION, whatever the contract says.

      There is no sell simulation on this chain. The service that actually tries to sell a token on your behalf has no coverage for Robinhood Chain, so honeypot detection rests on a source-code read alone. Every scan carries that warning. SAFE means "we found nothing that lets the creator take your money in the code we could read" — it does not mean the token will hold its value, and it is not a promise that a sell will go through.

      BLUE CHIP, and why it exists

      Robinhood Chain is itself younger than the age threshold the scanner uses for "established assets", so nothing on this chain can clear that gate — the canonical WETH scores CAUTION on a plain reading, because it is technically an upgradeable proxy with unlocked liquidity.

      BLUE CHIP is triggered by the deployer, not by a ticker list: the issuer that deployed every tokenised equity on the chain, the canonical bridged-asset deployer, and the USDG issuer. WETH and USDG themselves are canonical outright. On a blue chip the score is discarded entirely — "42 out of what?" has no honest answer for an asset like that — and every flag is restated as a note: the proxy becomes upgradeable proxy — standard for canonical and bridged assets, and the admin is the issuer.

      When a DANGER is downgraded

      A DANGER verdict with no measured hard finding (no honeypot, no tax, no pulled liquidity, no balance editing, no mint, no self-destruct), a clean creator, over $1M of liquidity and more than 30 days of age is downgraded to CAUTION, and it says so: downgraded from danger — $Nk of liquidity, N days old, and no honeypot, tax or pulled LP measured. The flags below are heuristics.

      "refused:" — the checks that run before your wallet opens

      Section 5

      5 · Limit orders

      Set a price and walk away. Your money never leaves your wallet, placing costs nothing, and the order can only ever execute on the terms you signed.

      The key property. A limit order is not an escrow. Nothing is deposited anywhere. You sign a message authorising exactly one transfer, on exactly one route, for exactly one minimum output — and until that fires, your tokens sit in your wallet and you can spend them on anything else. If The Desk disappeared tomorrow, an unfilled order would simply never fill.

      Placing one

      1. Open the LIMIT tab

        In the trade panel, or by pressing and holding the chart at the price you want — the panel opens with that price already filled in.

      2. Choose a side

        BUY / below market or SELL / above market. Switching resets the default distance: −20 % for a buy, +50 % for a sell.

      3. Set the trigger price

        Type a dollar price, or tap a percentage chip — −10 · −20 · −30 · −50 buying, +25 · +50 · +100 · +200 selling. The two are the same control seen two ways: typing a price updates the percentage and vice versa. The current price is shown next to the label.

      4. Set the amount

        Buying, the presets and MAX work exactly as in §4, with the same 0.0002 ETH gas reserve. Selling, the presets are 25 / 50 / 75 / 100 % of your position, defaulting to 50 %.

      5. Choose an expiry

        1h · 1d · 7d · 30d, defaulting to 7 days. The exact date and time it lapses is printed next to the label. 30 days is the maximum.

      6. Check you receive ≥

        This is the real commitment — the minimum output you are signing for. Buying, its dollar value is computed at your trigger price, not at today's price, because that is the price the order will fill at.

      7. Place it

        The button walks through what it needs. See below — there may be one or two preparation transactions the first time, then one signature and no transaction at all.

      You get: Order placed · buy below −20% · our keeper watches it.

      What actually gets signed, and what gets sent

      Buying with ETHLimit buys are funded in WETH, so if you do not already hold enough, the shortfall is wrapped in one transaction. Your ETH is wrapped when you sign and stays in your wallet until it fills.
      The Permit2 approvalOne approval, for the exact amount, to Permit2 — the standard allowance contract. This is the only place The Desk uses Permit2.
      The signatureAn EIP-712 typed message, not a transaction. It authorises a single transfer of a specific token, a specific amount, to a specific contract, with a specific deadline — and it carries the whole order inside it as a witness: your address, both tokens, the amount in, the minimum amount out, the expiry, the fee, the referrer, a random one-time nonce, and a hash of the exact swap route.
      Placing costs no gasAfter the one-off wrap and approval, placing an order is just the signature. Placing ten more costs nothing at all.
      Why the route is inside the signature. Without it, whoever executes your order could send it through a pool of their own and keep everything above your minimum. Because the route hash is part of what you signed, the contract rejects any execution that does not use exactly that route. The page recomputes that hash itself, from the route bytes, and refuses to sign if it does not match.

      How a fill happens

      An off-chain keeper watches the pool. When it will pay your minimum plus a small margin, the keeper simulates the execution and, if it passes, submits it. The contract then pulls your funds through Permit2 — validating your signature, your deadline and burning the one-time nonce — swaps along the route you signed, takes the fee you signed, and delivers the output to you.

      Only the keeper can executeThe execute function is restricted to allow-listed addresses, and even then it cannot deviate from what you signed.
      What you receiveOn this chain's V4 pools, a buy delivers the token and a sell delivers native ETH. On older V2/V3 pools a sell delivers WETH, which you unwrap yourself.
      The minimum, not the priceWhat is enforced is the minimum output you signed. The trigger price you typed is how the panel computes that minimum — it is not itself a term of the order. If pool depth changes dramatically, the price at which your minimum becomes achievable can differ from the price you typed.
      Why the chart can touch your price and the order still not fillThe chart draws the pool’s raw price; your order fills on the executable quote, which already carries the fee you signed (1%, or 0.9% referred) and the keeper’s 0.3% safety margin. In chart terms that is roughly 1.3% past your trigger. The fills near price on the ticket is the level that actually executes — trust that number, not the candle wick. And give the order room to live: a limit asking for a 5% move with a one-hour expiry usually dies of old age, not of malfunction.
      If it can never fillAfter enough consecutive failed simulations the order is marked FAILED rather than being retried forever. An order placed without the balance or the approval to back it is rejected outright at placement — an order that can never fill is not worth keeping.

      Cancelling

      You cancel the whole ladder, never a single rung. There is no per-rung cancel. One transaction returns the entire unfilled remainder to your wallet — as native ETH if that is what you funded it with.

      7 · Martingale

      Buy a first amount, then buy bigger every time the price drops a step, then sell the whole position once it is up by your target over the average cost.

      Understand the shape of this before you use it. Every level is larger than the last, so a sustained fall spends most of your budget near the top of the decline. It converts a series of small losses into one large one. The product's own risk line is: Each step buys more. If price never recovers +TP from average cost, the position stays open. It works on tokens that dip and bounce. It is ruinous on tokens that just go down.

      Martingale is listed in the bots hub but it runs from the trade panel: open a token and pick the MARTINGALE tab. It is long-only in this version — the SELL side is present but disabled, marked v1: long only.

      2. Level 0 buys at market

        On the keeper's first pass, with 3 % slippage allowed.

      3. Each further level waits for its price

        And this is enforced by the contract, not by the keeper: level k can only execute at or below the previous fill's price divided by (1 + step). The keeper cannot trigger a level early.

      4. Take-profit sells the lot

        The keeper quotes the whole position each pass. When it fetches at least what was spent plus your target, it sells everything and pays you in native ETH. The target is fixed in the contract at creation.

      5. With auto-restart, it goes again

        The level counter resets and the cycle continues on the unused budget, provided at least one more base-sized buy fits. Without it, the cycle closes and the remainder comes back.

                 upper = max(7-day high × 1.05, price × 1.03)

      Stop loss and take profit

      Both sell the entire position and close the bot. Neither is a partial exit.

      Setting themAt creation, as percentage chips with the resulting price shown underneath, or as a price you type. Spot Grid takes plain prices and requires the stop below the range and the take-profit above it.

      Fee-aware triggersThe prices are stored net of fees, so the 1 % and the pool fee are already inside the trigger — a stop at $0.10 means you receive as if the price were $0.10, not before costs.
      SpeedMeasured end to end at roughly 4–6 seconds from the price touching the stop to the sale being mined.
      Changing it laterPossible on Infinity Grid and DCA only, from the bot's detail page: one signature plus one transaction. Spot Grid's stop is immutable — the contract has no setter, so choose it carefully at creation.

Your funds are still safe and still withdrawable — but the protection you were counting on is not there. This is the single most important limitation of every bot on the platform.

      Why there is an oracle at all, and what stops it lying

      Chainlink's BTC/USD feed on Robinhood Chain republishes on a 0.5 % move or a 24-hour heartbeat. Measured on-chain, the median gap between updates is about 2.6 hours. Settling a five-minute round straight off it would print the same price at lock and at close nearly every time — every round a draw. So a keeper-fed oracle supplies the granularity.

      Chainlink is not bypassed; it is the fence. Publishing to the oracle runs this check on-chain, and reverts if it fails:

      PredictOracle.push — the bound
      deviation = |pushed − chainlink| ÷ chainlink
      if deviation > 10 % → revert OffAnchor
      The owner can tighten this but can never loosen it past 20 %.
      • The pushed price must be within 10 % of the live Chainlink answer or the transaction fails. The keeper cannot print a number; it can only fill in detail inside a band Chainlink vouches for.
      • If Chainlink itself is down, the push reverts too — there is no "anchor unavailable, publish anyway" path.
      • The oracle round id must strictly advance. Re-serving the same reading does not count as fresh, and the round is cancelled rather than settled twice on one number.
      • The keeper refuses to post at all unless at least two of its three exchange feeds are fresh. It will not settle a round on a single source.

      The fee and the payout, exactly

      Settlement math
      total  = UP pool + DOWN pool
      fee    = total × 3 %
      reward = total − fee

      your payout = your bet × reward ÷ (winning side's pool)
      The 3 % is taken on the whole pool — both sides — not on the winning side. The in-app explainer words this loosely ("a 3% fee on the winning pool"); the contract charges total × 3 %. This manual describes what the contract does.

      Draws, refunds and cancelled rounds

      How PnL is actually computed

      This matters more than it looks, because it is the difference between a number you can trust and a number that flatters you.

      MethodAverage cost. Every buy of a token rolls into one average entry price; a sell realises against that average. Not FIFO, not last-in.
      Where the trades come fromYour wallet's ERC-20 transfers, read from the block explorer. A transfer only counts as a trade if its transaction also emitted a Swap event — a transfer in from a friend is not a buy.
      Where the dollars come fromThe quote leg of that specific swap at the moment it happened — the ETH or USDG that actually moved, converted at the ETH price of that time. It is never a "price as of now" applied backwards to an old fill.
      Cost basisholding × avgCost, and only when both the average cost and the current price are known and positive.
      Unrealised %unrealised ÷ cost × 100.
      RealisedBooked at the moment of the sell, against the average cost at that moment.
      Fees and gasThe 1 % swap fee is taken out of the trade itself, so it is already inside the USD figures. Network gas is not tracked as a cost.
      No cost basis, no number. If a token arrived by airdrop, by transfer, or through a fill the index never saw, the cost cannot be established — so every PnL surface prints —. It is never quietly treated as a zero-cost buy, because that would print an infinite gain.
      It is recent PnL, not lifetime PnL. The wallet reader walks roughly the last 150 token transfers. A wallet with a longer history gets its most recent activity, so the totals are "what has been happening", not a career record.

      • A sell without a known entry is not booked as profit. That is the anti-inflation rule: buy somewhere else, sell through us, and the sale does not become a 100 % win. Only wallets with a closed trade rank on PNL at all.
      • Escrow fills count for the maker, never for the contract. A ladder or bot contract can never appear on the board with everybody's fills credited to it.
      • Referrer fees are the referrer's 30 % share of every fee a referred trader paid through the router inside the window.
      • Bots: public and in profit only. A private bot never appears, and a public bot with negative PnL does not appear either. Copying a public bot clones the strategy into your own escrow and pays its creator 30 % of your swap fees.
      • Everything is counted from Aug 21, 2026, the day the index started.

      Hook-fee pools

      Some V4 pools attach a "hook" — extra code that runs inside the swap and can keep part of the output. Measured on this chain, one such hook takes 2 % of every swap while its own published figure says 1 %.

      The Desk measures it rather than trusting itA tiny simulated swap is run against the pool to find the hook's real cut, cached for ten minutes, and folded into the pool fee. The hook's own declaration is only a fallback.
      In the trade panelThe cut is already subtracted from the quoted output before it reaches you, so a hook pool simply quotes worse. There is no separate line for it. If the hook could not be measured, the quote is marked est. rather than exact.
      In the botsIt is stated outright: this pool's hook takes 2.00 % on every swap (on top of the 1 % fee) — already subtracted from the net per grid, and it counts against the profitability guard.
      Why this matters for grids. A grid pays the fee on both legs. With a 1 % Desk fee, a pool fee and a 2 % hook, a round trip can be giving away 6 % before it makes anything — which is exactly why a grid on such a pool needs much wider levels, and why the guard will refuse the tight ones.

      The fee cannot move under you

      • The 1 % ceiling is hard-coded in the page. Any transaction carrying a higher fee is refused before your wallet opens, whatever the server says.
      • The router refuses anything below 0.9 % too, so nobody can use it for free at everyone else's expense.
      • A bot's fee rate is written into its record when it is created. A later change to the default does not reach a bot that is already running.
      • Limit orders carry their fee inside the signature. The rate you signed is the rate that gets charged.

      14 · Security & self-custody

      Three different custody shapes live on The Desk. Knowing which one you are in tells you exactly what could go wrong and exactly how you get your money back.

      Where your money actually is

      What you didWhere the money sitsHow you get it back
      Market buy / sellYour wallet. The swap is one atomic transaction: tokens in, tokens out, same block.Nothing to get back — it either happened or it reverted.
      Limit orderYour wallet. Nothing is escrowed; you signed a Permit2 witness that authorises exactly one transfer on exactly the terms you signed.Let it expire, cancel it, or revoke the Permit2 allowance. There is no balance to rescue.
      Ladder, Martingale, and the six bots (ETH- or USDG-quoted)An escrow contract on Robinhood Chain, booked to your wallet address as the maker.A maker-only withdrawal function that needs only your bot id. No keeper, no server, no route data, no expiry wait.
      Predict stakeThe Predict contract, until the round resolves.claim([epoch,…]) — winnings, or a full refund if the round was cancelled.

      The withdrawal guarantee

      Every escrow contract on The Desk exposes a function that does one thing: send everything in this record back to the wallet that owns it, right now, without selling anything. Four properties of that function are what the guarantee rests on, and all four were verified by direct on-chain calls on mainnet with no Desk API in the path:

      • It needs only the bot id. No route bytes, no quote, no signature from us. Grids and DCA take route = 0x and minOut = 0 because the route is only read inside the sellAll branch — and you pass sellAll = false.
      • It is maker-only. The contract compares msg.sender against the maker address recorded when the bot was created. Nobody can change that address, including us. Anyone else calling it reverts with NotMaker.
      • Pause does not block it. None of the withdrawal functions carry whenNotPaused. The pause switch stops the keeper's trading entries, nothing else.
      • Expiry does not block it, and expiry never forfeits anything. The withdrawal functions do not look at the date at all. After expiry a second path opens: refundExpired(id), callable by anybody — and it still pays the maker, never the caller. Someone else can push your funds home for you and cannot divert them.
      The deep dive is a separate page. Contract addresses, the exact function and field values for each bot type, a wallet-address bot-id finder that reads the chain directly, and a table of every revert message — The Desk · Bots: how to get your money out. It is a single self-contained file: save it with Ctrl+S / Cmd+S and it still works offline, and there is an off-site mirror on GitHub in case this domain goes away.

      What the operator can and cannot do

      Cannot move your escrowThe only owner-callable money function is rescue(token, to), and it is arithmetically capped at balanceOf(this) − escrowed[token] — the surplus above what users deposited. If that surplus is zero it reverts with NothingToRefund. There is no path that dips into user balances.
      Cannot redirect a withdrawalPayouts go to the maker address stored at creation. There is no setter for it.
      Cannot upgrade the contractsNo proxy, no upgrade mechanism. What is deployed is what runs, permanently. So none of the above can be changed later.
      Cannot raise the fee on a running botThe fee rate is written into your bot's record when you create it. A later change to the router's default does not reach it.
      Can pausePausing stops the keeper's swap entries. Withdrawals keep working. A paused bot is a frozen strategy, not frozen money.
      Ownership is two-steptransferOwnership → acceptOwnership, with a zero-address check, so a fat-fingered transfer cannot orphan the contract.

      What the keeper can and cannot do

      The keeper is the off-chain process that executes your bots. It runs on its own key (0x96D0B800…59246c) and it is not the owner of anything. Its entire power is "call the execute function on a record that already exists". Everything about what that execution is allowed to do is enforced by the contract:

      • It cannot sell a grid level at a loss. The profitable spacing is checked on-chain per level; a keeper transaction that would breach it reverts.
      • It cannot pick its own route on a limit order. The route is committed inside the order you signed as bytes32 routeHash, and execute() reverts with BadRouteHash if the submitted route does not hash to it. Before this was added, a keeper could route your fill through a pool of its own and keep whatever landed above your minAmountOut. It can't now.
      • It cannot beat your minimum. Every output is measured as the balance delta of the destination and compared to minAmountOut — not taken from what the pool claims to have returned. Fee-on-transfer tokens, rebasing tokens and hooks that skim part of the output are all caught by that check.
      • It cannot route you through an arbitrary V4 hook. A hook is attacker code running inside the router's unlock with the deltas open, so key.hooks must be address(0) or explicitly allowlisted.
      • It cannot make the router free for itself. The router enforces minFeeBps = 90 and reverts FeeTooLow() below it, and the referrer comes from the on-chain first-touch record, not from a parameter the caller chooses.
      • It cannot take custody. It never holds your funds at any point.
      The keeper's failure mode is inaction, not theft. If it goes down, your bot freezes mid-strategy: no buys, no sells, no take-profit and no stop-loss. Money is safe; the stop you were relying on is not. If the keeper stays down, withdraw and manage the position by hand — withdrawing does not need the keeper.

      Bounced ETH

      Every contract forwards ETH with a 30,000-gas stipend. If your wallet is a smart-contract wallet with an expensive receive(), the payment does not fail and the money is not lost — it is credited to pendingEth[you]. Read it with pendingEth(<your address>) and collect it with withdrawEth() (no arguments) on the same contract.

      The USDG grids use the token-shaped version of the same idea, because a regulated stablecoin can also refuse a transfer to a frozen address: the amount is credited to pending[you][token], read with pending(<your address>, <token>) and collected with withdrawPending(token, to) — which lets you name a different destination.

      Honest limits

      • The contracts have not been audited by a third party. They are verified and readable on Blockscout, and every withdrawal path in the rescue guide has been tested with real money on mainnet, but "no bug found" is never "no bug".
      • None of this protects the value of your position. A grid that bought all the way down hands you back exactly that: a losing position. Self-custody is a guarantee about access, not about price.
      • A hostile token can still hurt you. Transfer taxes, blacklists and mint switches live in the token contract, which The Desk does not control. The SAFE verdict on the token page flags what is detectable; it is a filter, not a promise.
      • Your keys are final. The maker address is permanent. Lose the wallet, lose the bot — there is no recovery path and we cannot build one.

      15 · FAQ & troubleshooting

      The things that actually go wrong, and what each one means.

      Wallet and network

      The connect button does nothing on my phone.

      You are almost certainly in Safari or Chrome. A wallet app's keys are not reachable from the phone's normal browser, so there is nothing for the page to connect to. Open nlyra.xyz inside your wallet app's own browser — every major wallet has one — and connect from there.

      The same applies to the block explorer's write forms if you ever use the self-withdrawal path: open the link inside the wallet app.

      "No wallet detected."

      The Desk uses injected wallets only — MetaMask, Rabby, or a wallet app's built-in browser. There is no WalletConnect and no e-mail login. Install one, or open the site inside your wallet app.

      "Switch your wallet to Robinhood Chain."

      Your wallet is on another network. The Desk offers to switch, and to add the network if you do not have it. If your wallet refuses the automatic switch, add it manually with the settings in §1 — chain id 4663.

      Everything re-checks the chain before building a transaction, so a wrong-network transaction can never be signed by accident.

      I have ETH but The Desk says I have none.

      Your ETH is on a different chain. ETH on Ethereum, Arbitrum or Base is not ETH on Robinhood Chain — it has to be bridged. See §1. This is the single most common thing that stops a new user.

      What is the "refused:" message?

      A safety check caught a mismatch between the transaction the server built and what is on your screen, before your wallet was opened. Nothing was signed and nothing was spent. It checks the destination contract, the chain, the tokens, the amounts, the minimum output, the fee and the referrer — against the raw calldata, not against the server's description of it.

      Re-quote and try again. If it persists, something is genuinely wrong and you should not push through it.

      Trading

      My transaction reverted. Did I lose the money?

      No. A reverted transaction does nothing at all — it is as if it never happened, except that you paid the gas. The usual cause is the price moving past your slippage between pressing the button and the block landing: Reverted — the price moved past your slippage. Nothing was taken.

      Try again, or raise slippage a step. On a fast-moving token 3 % is often not enough.

      "The price moved. Check the new number and tap again."

      The fresh quote differs from the one you were shown by more than 3 %, so the trade was stopped before it reached your wallet and the panel repainted with the real number. This is a feature: you are never asked to sign a trade whose terms changed after you decided.

      The button turned red and says BUY ANYWAY.

      One of two things. Either your order moves the pool by 10 % or more — in which case the trade needs a second press, and the arming lapses after six seconds — or the token carries a DANGER verdict with a deployer who has buried tokens before.

      Both are worth stopping for. The impact one is fixable by trading less; the other is not.

      Why did I get so much less than the price suggested?

      Three possible reasons, in order of likelihood:

      • Price impact. Your own order moved the pool. The quote line shows this from 1 % upward, and the @ price figure is the effective price at your size, not the headline one.
      • A hook pool. Some V4 pools take an extra 1–2 % on every swap. It is measured and already subtracted from the quote, so the quote was right — the pool is just expensive. See §13.
      • A transfer tax in the token itself. The SECURITY card on the INFO tab shows buy and sell tax when they are detectable.
      The price on a token that is not on the board looks wrong.

      Prices for tokens that are not on the market board come from a different path and can be thinner or slower. On this chain most V4 pools quote in native ether, and the ETH price itself has to be sourced separately — when that lookup fails, an off-board token can briefly price at zero rather than at a wrong number.

      If a figure looks impossible, check the pool on the INFO tab, look at the actual fills in the TRADES tab, and trust those over any derived figure.

      Is "cancel free" really free?

      There is no protocol fee, but cancelling a limit order or a ladder is a real on-chain transaction and you pay gas for it — around $0.001. It has to be on-chain: your signature already exists, and only the chain can void it.

      My limit order never filled even though the price hit it.

      What is enforced is the minimum output you signed, not the price you typed. If liquidity thinned out, the pool can print your price without being able to actually pay your minimum for your size.

      Other possibilities: the order expired; your balance or your Permit2 approval changed after placing it; or the keeper was down. Check the status on the order row — a FAILED row carries the reason.

      Bots

      "Not profitable — not created." Why won't it let me?

      Because the configuration cannot make money. A grid pays the 1 % fee on both legs plus the pool fee twice, so levels closer together than about 2.5 % net are guaranteed to lose. The rejection names your exact number and the maximum grid count for your range.

      Widen the range, use fewer grids, or pick a pool with a lower fee. The contract enforces the same floor independently, so this is not a setting we can waive.

      My grid is "Out of range" and has stopped trading.

      The price has left the range you set. Above it, everything was sold and the bot is sitting in ETH, missing the move. Below it, everything was bought and you are holding the token at a loss.

      Neither state loses your money by itself — you can stop the bot at any moment and take back either the ETH or the tokens. What you have lost is the opportunity. If you expect the price to come back, waiting is reasonable; if you do not, stop it.

      Infinity Grid has no upper bound and only stops at your floor, which is why it is the better choice when you have no view on the top of the range.

      My stop loss did not fire.

      Stop losses are executed by the keeper, not triggered by the contract on its own. If the keeper was down, nothing fired. The hub shows a keeper offline tag when that is the case.

      Your funds are safe and withdrawable regardless — but the protection was not there. This is the honest limitation of every automated exit on the platform. If the keeper is down and you were relying on a stop, withdraw and manage it by hand.

      Can I change a bot's parameters after starting it?

      Mostly no. You can add funds to a v3 grid, and you can change the stop loss on an Infinity Grid or a DCA. Everything else — the range, the grid count, the step, the take-profit — is fixed at creation, and Spot Grid's stop loss cannot be changed at all because the contract has no setter for it.

      To run different parameters, stop the bot and open a new one.

      Smart Rebalance says "Soon". When?

      No date. It is a placeholder — there is no contract and no engine behind it yet. It is visible in the product, which is why it is documented, but it does nothing.

      If I copy someone's bot, can they touch my money?

      No. A copy runs in your own escrow, from your own wallet, and only your wallet can withdraw it. What the creator gets is the referral: you pay 0.9 % instead of 1 %, and they earn 30 % of that fee.

      The strategy's parameters are hidden in the interface. They are, necessarily, inside the transaction you sign — so "hidden" means hidden in the product, not cryptographically secret.

      Predict

      My round says CANCELLED. Did I lose the bet?

      No — a cancelled round refunds every stake in full and the house takes nothing. Use the same CLAIM button. Rounds are cancelled when they cannot be settled honestly: a stale oracle, an oracle that did not advance, or a missed lock or close window.

      Past the close time plus the buffer, the contract makes your stake refundable by itself — no keeper and no human at The Desk is needed for you to get it back.

      The multiplier changed between betting and settling.

      That is how a parimutuel pool works: you split the losing side's stake, so the payout moves every time anyone bets, and it is only final at lock. A card showing 8× is telling you that almost nobody is on your side yet.

      The big price on screen is not the price it settled at.

      Correct, and deliberate. The line and the ticker come from an exchange socket in your own browser — fast, for reading. Settlement uses the on-chain oracle, which publishes once per round and is bounded by Chainlink: a price more than 10 % from Chainlink's own answer cannot be published at all. The header chip names both sources and turns amber if they drift.

      Polymarket refused my order.

      If it mentions your region, Polymarket geoblocks it — that is their restriction applied to your IP, and the order goes direct from your browser, so it is your location that counts. The NLYRA pool still works.

      Otherwise, check that you hold pUSD on Polygon (not USDC.e, not ETH), that both approvals are set, and that the order is at least 5 pUSD. The page tells you which step is missing.

      The screen

      The trade panel keeps folding itself on my iPhone.

      On a phone the panel docks at the bottom of the token page and collapses to a summary bar when it is scrolled away, so the chart keeps the fold. On a short screen — a small phone, or landscape — it stops pinning entirely rather than covering the whole page.

      Tap the bar to bring it back up. Turning the phone to landscape gives the chart the full screen; the banner and socials row hide themselves to make room.

      Numbers show "—" instead of a value.

      An em dash always means "we do not know", never zero. It is used deliberately: a holder count of 0, a PnL with no cost basis, a change window with no data, a buy-pressure figure with no flow. Printing a zero would be a claim we cannot support.

      PnL specifically shows — when the token arrived by airdrop or transfer, or from a fill the index never saw — there is no honest average cost to compare against.

      The live dot on the tape is amber.

      The push socket is down and the page has fallen back to polling. Everything still works, just a little behind. It reconnects on its own, and every screen keeps a polling fallback underneath precisely so the socket is never the only way something updates.

      The board stopped updating while I was scrolling.

      On purpose. Once you have scrolled past the top, rows stop reordering under your finger — prices keep updating in place, and a pill appears saying N new tokens. Tap it, or scroll back to the top, to let the new ordering in.

      My trade does not show in MY BAG.

      Give it a few seconds — the wallet read is cached for 30 seconds and refreshed shortly after a confirmed trade.

      If it still does not appear, check the dust filter: positions under $0.50 are hidden by default, with a show dust button in the totals strip.

      Martingale positions never appear in MY BAG — those tokens are in the escrow contract, not your wallet. The cycle card carries their cost basis instead.

      Money and safety

      Can The Desk take my money?

      Market and limit orders never place your money anywhere we could reach. For the escrowed strategies, the only administrative money function on the contracts is arithmetically capped at the surplus above what users deposited and reverts if it would touch a user balance. Withdrawals pay the address recorded at creation, which has no setter. The contracts have no upgrade mechanism, so none of that can be changed later.

      You do not have to take our word for it: the contracts are verified and readable on the block explorer. See §14 and /desk/help/bots.

      What if The Desk disappears?

      Unfilled limit orders simply never fill — nothing to recover. Escrowed strategies are withdrawn directly from the contract with your wallet, your bot id and a block explorer, needing no server of ours. Predict stakes on unsettled rounds become refundable by the contract on their own.

      The full procedure lives at /desk/help/bots. It is one self-contained file — save it to your device now, while everything is fine.

      Is SAFE a guarantee?

      No. SAFE means the contract was read and nothing was found that lets the creator take your money. It says nothing about whether the token holds its value, and on this chain there is no sell simulation available — honeypot detection rests on a source-code read alone.

      Most tokens on any chain go to zero on their own, without anyone doing anything wrong.
